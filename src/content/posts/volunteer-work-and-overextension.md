This is a placeholder for a more personal post about the last stretch of my academic life and why I’m stepping back from a lot of volunteering and student org obligations.

I overextended myself. That is the simplest version of it. I wanted to say yes to too many things at once: workshops, teaching sessions, logistics, events, mentoring, and all the behind-the-scenes work that keeps a student org functioning. I wanted to help, and I genuinely cared about the work, but I also spread myself too thin.

The cost was not just in my calendar. It showed up in my classes, my energy, and my ability to actually focus on the work that mattered most to me. I was trying to build community and add value, but I wasn’t giving enough attention to the things that would help me grow technically and academically in the long run.

I’ve come away from that experience with a clearer idea of my priorities. I still care about helping people learn practical engineering skills. I still think student organizations can be a powerful place for that. But I also know I need to be more careful about how much I take on and how I choose what to say yes to.

The lesson is not “don’t help people.” It’s “help people in a way that is sustainable.”

I’m choosing more intentionally now. My current path is to focus on my classes, protect time for real technical work, and invest in the projects that are most aligned with the kind of engineering work I want to do after graduation. That means being more disciplined about time, commitment, and goals. It also means accepting that saying no is part of doing good work.

This post will eventually be expanded into a more personal reflection on burnout, service work, and how I’m re-centering around engineering and personal growth.
